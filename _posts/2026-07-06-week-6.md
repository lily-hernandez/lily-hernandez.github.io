During the sixth week, I began preparing the physiological signal data for machine learning. The main goal of this stage is to build a model that can classify whether a signal represents pain or no pain.

One challenge is that the physiological signal vectors do not all have the same length. Most machine-learning algorithms require each observation to contain the same number of input values. To solve this problem, I first identified the maximum signal length in the dataset.

I then planned to pad every shorter signal vector with zeros until all vectors matched the maximum length. This creates a consistent input shape while preserving the original signal measurements.

The pain and no-pain conditions will be used as the classification labels. The exact experimental design will be determined based on the findings from the earlier statistical and visual analyses. Possible next steps include dividing the data into training and testing sets, selecting an appropriate classification algorithm, training the model, and evaluating its accuracy.

This stage connects the earlier data-processing and feature-analysis work to the final goal of automatically distinguishing pain from no pain using physiological signals.
