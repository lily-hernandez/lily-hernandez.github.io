During the fourth week, I created eight boxplots in one figure using Python. Each boxplot represented one physiological signal type under either the pain or no-pain condition.

The final graph included four boxplots for pain and four boxplots for no pain. Boxplots are useful because they show the median, spread, range, and possible outliers within a dataset.

This visualization made it easier to compare the distributions of the physiological signals. Signals with larger boxes and longer whiskers showed greater variability, while smaller boxes represented signals with values that were more closely grouped together.

One challenge was that the physiological signals were measured on different numerical scales. Because of this, some signals appeared much larger than others when displayed on the same graph. Even with this limitation, the boxplots provided a useful summary of the differences between the pain and no-pain conditions.
